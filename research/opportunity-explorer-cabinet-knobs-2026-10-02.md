## Overview

This category does not look as strong for ARCU's first wholesale/FBA experiment as the wrench family.

The physically attractive profile is real: cabinet hardware is small, light, durable, non-electrical, and easy to ship. But the Amazon niche data shows a different problem: the higher-priced subsets generally have **double-digit return rates**, while the lower-return rows tend to have ASPs that are too low for ARCU's absolute-dollar contribution target.

## Interpretation

### Higher-ASP subsets

The best-looking price/growth rows are:

- `champagne bronze cabinet pulls`
- `polished nickel cabinet knobs`
- `unlacquered brass cabinet knob`
- `champagne bronze cabinet knobs`

All have ASPs around $26–$34 and positive search growth.

However, all four show return rates of roughly **10.5%–11.7%**.

That is materially worse than the roughly 2%–3% return rates observed in the wrench niches. For a first FBA experiment, this increases effective cost, handling complexity, and margin uncertainty.

Likely drivers include finish/color mismatch, style preference, size/fit expectations, multipack count, and installation compatibility.

### Broad cabinet hardware

The large generic rows have massive demand but weaker economics:

- `cabinet handles` — $23.69 ASP, 119 top products
- `cabinet knobs` — $16.27 ASP, 95 top products

These are likely too broad and price-competitive for a small wholesale seller without a sourcing advantage.

### Small niche exception

`unlacquered brass cabinet knob` is the cleanest specialist signal:

- $30.77 ASP
- +25.49% 180-day search growth
- 1.5k–2k units/year
- only 39 top-clicked products

But its **10.47% return rate** remains high enough that it does not justify deeper brand/supplier work yet.

## Decision

**Reject cabinet knobs / cabinet hardware as a priority first-experiment category.**

Reason:

1. The attractive ASP/growth rows carry unusually high return rates.
2. Generic rows have low ASPs and broad competition.
3. The likely market is heavily style/finish/private-label driven rather than brand-direct wholesale friendly.
4. The first experiment should favor cleaner economics and fewer customer-fit/preferences variables.

Do not spend time exporting detail tabs for this category unless a specific authorized brand opportunity appears independently.

Next move: return to the Discover Unmet Demand Beta list and test a category with a materially different demand/supply structure rather than continuing deeper into decorative cabinet hardware.
