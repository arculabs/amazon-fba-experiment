## Primary candidate for deeper niche inspection: torque wrench

The generic `torque wrench` niche is the strongest first detail screen:

- **4.57m searches/year**
- **125k–150k units/year**
- **$49.45 average selling price**
- **31 top-clicked products**
- **3.28% return rate**
- 180-day search growth remains positive at **+5.09%**

This is a much better economic shape than the low-ASP categories previously screened.

It is **not yet a sourcing candidate**. The next detail screen must establish:

- top-click concentration / dominant brands;
- Amazon Retail / Amazon Basics presence;
- exact high-volume ASINs;
- price distribution rather than average only;
- product size/weight and FBA fee burden;
- whether brand-direct/authorized wholesale supply is realistically obtainable.

## Secondary signal: bike torque wrench

`bike torque wrench` deserves a second look because:

- **+51.37%** 180-day search growth;
- 12.5k–15k units/year;
- **$45.29** average price;
- only 23 top-clicked products.

However, its **3.68% return rate** is somewhat higher and the niche may be dominated by enthusiast brands / kit-style products.

## Decision

**Advance the generic `torque wrench` niche to detailed inspection.**

Do not approach suppliers yet. First open/export the niche detail page for the generic `torque wrench` row so we can inspect top clicked ASINs, brand concentration, conversion behavior, and product mix.

### The wholesale-access problem

The strongest warning is seller structure:

- **26 of 31** top products average only **2 sellers/vendors**
- Amazon Basics averages **1**
- only four exported ASINs show materially broader seller participation:
  - CRAFTSMAN 1/2-inch — **11**
  - CRAFTSMAN 3/8-inch — **18**
  - DEWALT set — **9**
  - TEKTON split-beam — **11**

Those four multi-seller ASINs together represent only about **11.32%** of niche click share.

Interpretation: the market itself is economically attractive, but most of the demand is captured by tightly controlled / DTC-style brands rather than open wholesale listings. That is exactly the distinction the market-first process was intended to expose before supplier work.

## First brand-access screen

### TEKTON — promising brand relationship, exact top ASIN not suitable

TEKTON publicly accepts a limited number of Authorized Dealers and explicitly offers:

- wholesale pricing;
- resale access to all products through its dealer portal;
- purchase by **each** rather than mandatory case packs;
- an average expectation of **$5,000/year** in dealer purchases;
- $15 standard shipping below $300;
- minimum advertised pricing enforcement.

This is unusually compatible with ARCU's desired brand-direct model.

However, the exact TEKTON ASIN surfaced in this niche — `B0B4BFXNJ9`, item `TRQ62203` — is not a clean replenishable candidate. TEKTON's current product page shows that model unavailable/out of stock, while current replacement/newer torque-wrench models are sold separately. Do not model the old ASIN as a replenishable first SKU.

The correct next question for TEKTON is **channel authorization**, not price: does its Authorized Dealer program permit third-party Amazon Marketplace resale, and can it provide the invoice/authorization chain Amazon may require?

If yes, current TEKTON models should be searched by exact ASIN in Product Opportunity Explorer and screened individually before asking for dealer pricing.

### CRAFTSMAN / DEWALT — open listings, but less attractive first relationship

CRAFTSMAN and DEWALT have multiple sellers on the surfaced ASINs, which indicates a more open reseller structure. But these products are comparatively heavy, competition is broader, and public Amazon pricing on the CRAFTSMAN 1/2-inch model has shown meaningful volatility. They remain possible sourcing paths, but are not as clean a first brand-direct experiment as a manufacturer with an explicit small-dealer program.

### LEXIVON / EPAuto / other top DTC-style brands

LEXIVON and EPAuto dominate demand but the exported ASINs average only two sellers/vendors. No clear public authorized-dealer program surfaced in the first brand-access check. Treat them as **brand-controlled until proven otherwise** rather than assuming the listings are wholesale-accessible.

## Updated decision

The generic torque-wrench niche is **MARKET-VALIDATED BUT SOURCING-CONSTRAINED**.

Do not reject the niche, but do not start ordinary distributor hunting.

Next bounded step:

1. test TEKTON's Authorized Dealer path specifically for **Amazon Marketplace permission**;
2. if Amazon resale is allowed, identify current/replenishable TEKTON ASINs and run exact Opportunity Explorer economics;
3. if Amazon resale is not allowed or the wholesale spread is insufficient, return to Opportunity Explorer and continue with the next market-first niche rather than forcing torque wrenches.
