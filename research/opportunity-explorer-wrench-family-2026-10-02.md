## Interpretation

### Ratcheting wrench set

This is the largest market by far and has an excellent $57.24 ASP, but the niche is broad:

- over 5m searches/year;
- 125k–150k units/year;
- **103 top-clicked products**;
- search volume down **9.43%** over 180 days.

This likely contains many brands, set sizes, tooth counts, flex/non-flex variants, metric/SAE configurations, and price tiers. Strong market, but too heterogeneous to treat as a single sourcing target without detailed product/brand inspection.

### Crows foot wrench set

This is the cleanest next niche to inspect:

- **470,678 searches/year**
- essentially flat 180-day demand (**+0.22%**)
- **15k–20k units/year**
- only **30 top-clicked products**
- **$51.34 ASP**
- **2.39% returns**

It has enough demand and ASP for meaningful per-unit contribution, while the product universe is much tighter than ratcheting wrench sets. The main likely risk is brand concentration / set weight.

### Flare nut wrench set

Also attractive:

- 327,888 searches/year;
- **+8.63%** 180-day growth;
- 15k–20k units/year;
- 37 top-clicked products;
- $32.11 ASP;
- 2.10% return rate.

The ASP is closer to ARCU's lower target boundary, so sourcing cost will matter more, but the demand structure is healthy enough for a detailed look.

### Metric wrench sets

Reasonable but less compelling:

- $39.75 ASP;
- 15k–20k units/year;
- 50 top products;
- mild demand contraction.

Keep as secondary.

### Flex-head ratcheting wrench set

Very attractive ASP and low returns, but:

- search demand is down 12.44%;
- 109 top-clicked products makes the niche highly fragmented.

Worth revisiting only after simpler wrench niches are exhausted.

## Decision

Advance **`crows foot wrench set`** to detailed niche inspection first.

Why:

1. ASP is high enough at **$51.34**.
2. Demand is meaningful at **15k–20k units/year**.
3. Search demand is essentially stable.
4. Only **30 top-clicked products**, which should make brand and ASIN concentration easier to assess than the 103-product ratcheting-wrench niche.
5. Product type is durable, non-electrical, and mechanically simple.

If crows-foot sourcing is too concentrated or inaccessible, inspect **`flare nut wrench set`** next.

Do not start supplier outreach from this broad scan alone. First inspect detailed Products and Search Terms tabs for the selected niche.

### ASP reality check

The broad niche screen showed an average price of **$51.34**, but the detailed product mix is less attractive than that headline suggests.

Across the 30 exported ASINs:

- click-share-weighted ASP is approximately **$41.10**
- median ASIN ASP is approximately **$41.27**
- products below $30 capture roughly **29.93%** of niche click share
- products from $30 to $60 capture roughly **56.33%**
- products above $60 capture roughly **13.58%**

The highest-volume ASIN is DURATECH at only **$30.18**, and the second is WORKPRO at **$19.52**. High-priced jumbo sets and TEKTON's $299.73 set pull the broad niche average upward.

- average selling price: **$43.76**

GEARWRENCH publicly lists a customer-contact route and recognizes distributor relationships, but no self-service dealer application was found in the initial pass. Channel permission should be confirmed directly before pricing work.

## Updated decision

The crows-foot niche is **MARKET-VALIDATED BUT MOSTLY SOURCING-CONSTRAINED**.

Do not pursue the dominant DURATECH / WORKPRO / private-label-style listings.

The only exact ASIN worth a bounded access/economics check now is **GEARWRENCH 81908**. If GEARWRENCH permits authorized Amazon Marketplace resale and wholesale cost leaves at least the current ~$5/unit contribution floor after FBA/inbound/prep, then this could become a first-order candidate.

SUNEX remains a secondary supplier-access lead, but its surfaced 9710M ASP is probably too low to justify outreach before GEARWRENCH.

If GEARWRENCH access/economics fail, move to the previously identified **`flare nut wrench set`** niche rather than spending more time on crows-foot listings.
