## Interpretation

### Generic tortilla press

This is the only row worth drilling into:

- **3.8m searches/year**
- **125k–150k units/year**
- **$36.11 ASP**
- only **12 top-clicked products**
- **5.08% return rate**
- 90-day demand essentially flat at **+0.31%**
- 180-day demand down **21.52%**

The long-run decline is a caution, but the recent flattening means it may be stabilizing rather than continuing to fall at the same rate.

The unusually small 12-product click set is attractive for research efficiency: a detail export should quickly show whether demand is concentrated in established brands such as Victoria or in private-label sellers.

### Victoria tortilla press

The brand-specific row is useful as a signal:

- $50.93 ASP
- 2k–2.5k units/year
- one top-clicked product
- 4.0% return rate

However, search demand is down sharply over both 180 and 90 days. Treat Victoria as a potential exact brand/ASIN lead inside the generic niche rather than as a separate growth opportunity.

### Wood tortilla press

Reject as a separate niche. ASP is workable, but demand is weaker, returns are slightly higher, and the product set is more fragmented.

### Electric tortilla / roti-maker rows

Do not pursue. Their $371–$546 average prices are clearly driven by a mixed product set that reaches above $2,000, and the category introduces electrical/mechanical complexity outside the preferred first-experiment profile.

## Decision

Advance only the exact **`tortilla press`** niche to detailed **Products + Search Terms** inspection.

Primary gates:

1. whether Victoria or another established brand dominates;
2. seller participation on the high-click ASINs;
3. actual item weights, because cast-iron presses may weaken FBA economics;
4. whether the $36.11 niche ASP is supported by the dominant ASINs;
5. whether private-label concentration makes authorized wholesale access impractical.

If the dominant branded/open listings are physically heavy or insufficiently priced, reject the niche and move to `moka pot`.
